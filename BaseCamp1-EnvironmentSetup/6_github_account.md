# Creating Your GitHub Account

GitHub is where code lives: it stores your projects, keeps a full history of every change, and lets you share your work with instructors, teammates, and future employers. The account is free and takes about ten minutes. Set it up now so you can start saving fellowship projects to it as soon as they begin.

🐙 github.com ⏱ ~10 minutes Free plan is all you need

Why this matters

## Your public developer identity

Your GitHub profile is a portfolio: every project you publish appears under your username, and many developer tools let you sign in with GitHub instead of creating yet another password. You'll need:

  * ✓ **A personal email address** you check regularly — GitHub emails you a code to confirm it.
  * ✓ **A password manager** (or a safe place) for a strong, unique password.
  * ✓ **A smartphone** with an authenticator app, or a passkey-capable device, for two-factor authentication in Step 4.

No card or payment details are needed. GitHub's free plan covers everything in this fellowship, including unlimited public and private repositories.

Step 1 — Decide first

## Choose a Username You'll Be Happy With

Your username appears in the URL of everything you publish — `github.com/your-username` — and on your commits. It can be changed later, but links and references may break when it does, so pick one you'd be comfortable putting on a résumé.

  * It may contain letters, numbers, and single hyphens only.
  * It can't start or end with a hyphen, or contain two hyphens in a row.
  * It can be at most 39 characters.
  * It must be unique — if your first choice is taken, try adding your surname, a number, or a hyphen.

A simple, professional choice like `firstname-lastname` ages better than a nickname or a joke.

Step 2 — Create the account

## Sign Up with Your Email

sign-up pageCopy
[code] 
    https://github.com/signup
[/code]

  1. **Enter your email address** Use the personal email you'll keep long-term.
  2. **Create a password** At least 15 characters of any kind, _or_ at least 8 characters that include a number and a lowercase letter. Longer is better — let your password manager generate one.
  3. **Enter your username** The one you chose in Step 1. GitHub tells you immediately if it's already taken.
  4. **Choose whether to receive product emails** Optional — you can say no.
  5. **Solve the verification puzzle** GitHub uses a short visual puzzle to confirm you're a person.
  6. **Click "Create account"** GitHub emails you a launch code — continue to Step 3.

GitHub also offers **Continue with Google** on the sign-up page. It works, but signing up with an email and password makes it easier to understand exactly how your account is secured — and easier to recover if you later lose access to a social login.

Step 4 — Secure the account

## Turn On Two-Factor Authentication

Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds a second check on top of your password. GitHub recommends it for everyone and requires it for accounts that contribute code, so it's best to enable it now.

  1. **Open your account settings** Click your profile picture at the top right, then choose **Settings**.
  2. **Go to "Password and authentication"** It's in the left-hand sidebar under Access.
  3. **Click "Enable two-factor authentication"**
  4. **Choose your method** An **authenticator app** (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy, or a password manager) or a **passkey / security key**. Follow the prompts, scanning the QR code if you use an app.
  5. **Download and store your recovery codes** These are your way back in if you lose your phone. Save them in your password manager — not in a note on the same phone.

Recovery codes matter If you lose your 2FA device and your recovery codes, you can be permanently locked out of the account. Store the codes before you close this settings page.

Step 5 — Optional but recommended

## Protect Your Email Address

Every commit you make is stamped with an email address, and on a public repository, anyone can read it. GitHub can substitute a private "noreply" address instead.

  1. **Open Settings → Emails**
  2. **Tick "Keep my email addresses private"** Your commits will use a GitHub-provided noreply address rather than your real one.
  3. **Optionally tick "Block command line pushes that expose my email"** This stops you accidentally pushing commits that contain your real address.

While you're in Settings, you can also add your name and a profile photo under **Public profile**. It's not required, but a profile with a real name looks more credible when you share it.

Troubleshooting

## If Something Goes Wrong

  * **Username is taken:** add a hyphen, a number, or your surname. Remember the rules from Step 1.
  * **Password rejected:** it must be at least 15 characters, or at least 8 characters including a number and a lowercase letter.
  * **No launch code email:** check spam, wait a couple of minutes, then use the resend option. Confirm you typed the email address correctly.
  * **Can't complete the verification puzzle:** try again, use the audio option if offered, or switch browsers or disable extensions that might interfere.
  * **Lost your 2FA device:** use one of your saved recovery codes to sign in, then set up 2FA on your new device.

Still stuck? Tell your instructor before Base Camp 2. Never share your password, 2FA codes, or recovery codes with anyone.
