Base Camp 1 · Week 1 · Cloud Account

Creating Your AWS Account

Amazon Web Services (AWS) is where the fellowship's projects will eventually be deployed. Creating an account takes about fifteen minutes, but it involves email codes, a card, and a phone verification — so do it now, calmly, rather than during a live session. You'll sign up with your email address, and that same email becomes your root user login.

☁️ Amazon Web Services ⏱ ~15 minutes 💳 Credit or debit card required

Why this matters

Your email is your root login

When you create an AWS account, the email address and password you sign up with become the root user — the one identity with complete access to everything in the account, including billing. Signing in later as the root user simply means entering that same email and password.

Have these ready before you start:

  • ✓ A personal email address you'll keep for years and can open right now — AWS emails you a verification code.
  • ✓ A phone number that can receive a text message (SMS) or a voice call.
  • ✓ A credit or debit card with international payments enabled. AWS places a small temporary hold to verify it.
  • ✓ A password manager (or a safe place) to store the root password you're about to create.
Choose the email carefully Don't use a work or college address you might lose access to — if you lose the root email, recovering the account becomes painful. Also avoid an address shared with other people.

Start Sign-Up: Email, Account Name & Password

sign-up page
https://portal.aws.amazon.com/billing/signup

You can also go to aws.amazon.com and choose Create an AWS Account — both land on the same sign-up flow.

  1. Enter your root user email addressThis is the personal email from the checklist above.
  2. Enter an AWS account nameA friendly label for your own reference, e.g. AI Accelerator Hub-fellowship. It's not your login and can be changed later.
  3. Click "Verify email address"AWS sends a verification code to that inbox.
  4. Enter the code and click "Verify"Check your spam or promotions folder if it doesn't arrive within a minute or two.
  5. Create your root user passwordUse uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols. Make it long, unique, and store it in your password manager — this password can control your entire account and its billing.
  6. Click "Continue"The sign-up wizard walks you through the remaining screens.

  1. Choose "Personal" as the account typePersonal and Business accounts work identically — the choice only changes what information AWS asks for. Personal is right for coursework.

Step 5 of 8

Choose Your Account Plan & Support Plan

Newer AWS accounts choose between a Free plan and a Paid plan. New accounts receive $100 in credits at sign-up, and can earn up to $100 more by completing activities in the console.

Free planPaid plan
ChargesNone while on the planPay-as-you-go beyond your credits
ServicesA select set of AWS servicesAll AWS services
Lasts6 months, or until credits run out — whichever is firstOngoing
When it endsAccount closes; upgrade within 90 days to keep itAccount stays open
  1. Choose the Free planIt's the safe default for learning — it's designed so you can't run up a bill by accident. If a later exercise needs a service the Free plan excludes, your instructor will tell you, and you can upgrade at any time from the console.
  2. Choose "Basic support"It's free, and the default. You can change support tiers later.
  3. Click "Complete sign up"AWS now activates your account.
Set a calendar reminder If you stay on the Free plan, your account closes after six months or when the credits are used up. Note the date now — you'll be able to check your remaining credits and plan status on the Billing and Cost Management home page. AWS adjusts these offers periodically, so trust the on-screen wording if it differs from this page.

Step 6 of 8

Sign In as the Root User

Activation usually takes a few minutes (occasionally longer). AWS emails you when it's ready — then sign in with the email and password you just created.

console sign-in
https://console.aws.amazon.com/
  1. Choose "Root user"If your browser shows an IAM user sign-in page instead, click Sign in using root user email.
  2. Enter your root user email address and click "Next"This is the same email you signed up with.
  3. Complete the security check if promptedType the characters exactly as shown, without spaces.
  4. Enter your root user password
  5. Complete MFA if you've enabled it, then click "Sign in"You'll set up MFA in the next step.
You'll land on the Console Home page. The Region selector is at the top right — use the region your instructor names for course exercises, since resources you create belong to one region.

Step 7 of 8

Secure the Root User with MFA

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) means a stolen password alone can't open your account. Do this now — it takes two minutes.

  1. Open Security credentialsClick your account name at the top right of the console, then choose Security credentials.
  2. Find "Multi-factor authentication (MFA)" and click "Assign MFA device"
  3. Name the device and choose "Authenticator app"A passkey or security key also works if you have one.
  4. Scan the QR code with an authenticator appGoogle Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy, or a password manager with one-time-code support.
  5. Enter two consecutive codes and click "Add MFA"Wait for the app to show a fresh code between the two entries.
Don't lock yourself out If you lose your phone, you lose the MFA device. Keep your account contact details current, and consider registering a second MFA device as a backup.
Root user: use sparingly AWS recommends that you don't use the root user for everyday work — keep it for account-level tasks only. For day-to-day exercises, your instructor will guide you to create a separate, limited user; until then, signing in as root to look around is fine.

Step 8 of 8

Set a Zero-Spend Budget Alert

A cheap second safety net: AWS emails you the moment your account starts accruing charges, so a forgotten resource never becomes a surprise bill.

  1. Search for "Budgets" in the console's top search barOpen it under Billing and Cost Management.
  2. Click "Create budget"
  3. Choose "Use a template (simplified)" → "Zero spend budget"
  4. Enter a budget name and your email address, then click "Create budget"Use an email you actually read.
On the Free plan you're protected by design, but if you ever upgrade to the Paid plan, this alert becomes your early warning. Setting it up now costs nothing.

Troubleshooting

If Something Goes Wrong

  • No verification email: check spam and promotions, wait a couple of minutes, and confirm you typed the address correctly. Use the resend option on the verification screen.
  • Card declined: confirm the billing address matches your bank's records, that international online payments are enabled, and that the card supports online use. A different card often solves it.
  • Phone verification fails: try the voice-call option instead of SMS (or the reverse), and make sure the number includes the correct country code.
  • Can't sign in / "wrong email or password": make sure you chose Root user and not IAM user. If you forgot the password, use the "Forgot password?" link on the sign-in page.
  • Account still activating: it can take a little while. Wait for AWS's confirmation email before assuming something failed.
Still stuck? Tell your instructor before Base Camp 2 — and don't share your password, verification codes, or card details with anyone in the process, including in class chat.
